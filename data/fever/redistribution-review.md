# FEVER redistribution review

## Scope

The snapshot contains the complete labelled shared-task development split of FEVER, the published file `shared_task_dev.jsonl` whose SHA-256 digest is `e89865bfe1b4dd054e03dd57d7241a6fde24862905f31117cf0cd719f7c78df7`: 19,998 claims, each with its label and, where one was recorded, every evidence set an annotator marked. Beside each evidence address the snapshot reproduces the sentence it points at, taken from the authors' own pre-processed Wikipedia archive, whose SHA-256 digest is `4b06d95da6adf7fe02d2796176c670dacccb21348da89cba4c50676ab99665f2`. Only the 7,100 cited sentences of the 2,892 cited pages are copied; the other five million pages of that archive are not. The training split, the unlabelled development file, the blind test split, and the rest of the processed corpus are not imported. Both the public repository and the website publish this content. No code or images are copied. [Dataset page](https://fever.ai/dataset/fever.html)

The terms on Wikipedia's article pages are Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike, currently version 4.0 and version 3.0 before June 2023, so both branches of that sentence point at the same family of terms: copying, redistribution, public display, and adaptation are permitted when the work is credited, a link to the terms is given, notices are kept, changes are indicated, and adaptations are shared under the same terms. Adaptations of CC BY-SA 3.0 material may be published under version 4.0, so this snapshot carries a share-alike notice naming both. The files are served over plain HTTPS with no registration, access gate, or click-through agreement; only submitting a system for a blind-test score requires a CodaLab account, which this import does not do. [Notice](https://fever.ai/download/fever/license.html) and [legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/legalcode)

The claims were written and labelled by crowdworkers hired by the authors, by rewriting sentences from the introductions of about 50,000 popular English Wikipedia pages in the dump of June 2017. The evidence sentences this snapshot reproduces are Wikipedia prose, so they carry Wikipedia's own terms, which the notice above defers to; Wikipedia's reuse guidance accepts a link to the article as credit to its contributors. FEVER records each evidence sentence's page title but not the revision it came from, so attribution can only point at the article: the generated `ATTRIBUTION.md` lists all 2,892 cited articles with links, and each reference solution names the page every sentence comes from. [Wikipedia's reuse terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reusing_Wikipedia_content) and [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05355)

A scan of the imported text found no contact details, identifiers, or other personal data beyond what Wikipedia records about notable people, organisations, and places; the claims are statements about the subjects of popular Wikipedia articles. The crowdworkers who wrote and labelled the claims are not identified anywhere in the data.

## Changes

Each task prompt is the claim, verbatim and unaccompanied: no instruction is added, since the claim is what the dataset gives a system. The reference solution is the label as upstream spells it, `SUPPORTS`, `REFUTES`, or `NOT ENOUGH INFO`, followed by the evidence sets. Each set is listed on its own, one line per sentence giving the page title, the sentence number as upstream records it counting from zero, and the sentence. Page titles keep the upstream form, in which spaces are underscores and brackets and colons are written `-LRB-`, `-RRB-`, and `-COLON-`, and the sentences keep the tokenised spacing of the authors' processed corpus, which puts a space before punctuation and writes brackets the same way. The claims with no recorded evidence, which are exactly the 6,666 labelled `NOT ENOUGH INFO`, carry the label alone.

Two mechanical steps were needed. The upstream evidence list repeats a set once per annotator who chose it, so identical sets are written once: the largest claim falls from 116 recorded sets to 32 distinct ones. And 32 of the 2,892 cited page titles are written in a different Unicode normalisation in the claims file than in the processed corpus, so pages are matched by their normalised titles; the titles in the solutions are the ones the claims file records. The upstream `verifiable` field is not reproduced, because it duplicates the label: it reads `NOT VERIFIABLE` for exactly the claims labelled `NOT ENOUGH INFO`. The internal annotation and evidence identifiers that the dataset page describes as "for internal use only and not used for scoring" are not reproduced. Keys are the upstream claim identifiers. Prompt and solution links point at the pinned split file, which has no per-claim addresses, and the environment link is omitted.

## Notices

FEVER is from "FEVER: a Large-scale Dataset for Fact Extraction and VERification" by James Thorne, Andreas Vlachos, Christos Christodoulopoulos, and Arpit Mittal, NAACL-HLT 2018. The claims, labels, and evidence annotations were written by crowdworkers.

The evidence sentences are English Wikipedia prose written by Wikipedia contributors, taken from the dump of June 2017. All 2,892 cited articles are credited by title and linked in the `ATTRIBUTION.md` that accompanies this snapshot, and every solution names the page each sentence comes from. FEVER does not record which revision of each article its sentences came from.
